YOUR FIRST WORKOUT
Quick guide — takes 30 seconds to read
WARM UP 1, WARM UP 2
Light sets to prepare the muscle and joint. Enter your warmup weight and reps — the app will automatically calculate your working weight from your warmup. Tap ✓ when done.
1, 2, 3 — WORKING SETS
These are your main sets. The weight field is pre-filled with your target based on last session. Enter how many reps you actually completed, then tap ✓. The rest timer starts automatically.
PROGRESSIVE OVERLOAD
Hit 8+ reps → next session weight goes up 2.5kg. Hit fewer than 6 → weight drops 2.5kg. The app tracks this automatically. Just log honestly.
FINISH WORKOUT
Hit the gold FINISH WORKOUT button at the bottom when all sets are done. Your streak and history are saved.

FIRST SESSION TIP
Pick any weight — even light
For your first session, don't worry about the right weight. Just grab something manageable and go.
The rule: If you hit 20+ reps on a set, the weight is too light. Your coach will automatically suggest heavier next session.
If you hit 6–12 reps — that's your working range. The app will build progressive overload from there.
If you hit fewer than 6 — weight is too heavy. Drop it next session, no shame in that.
After one real session, the app knows your strength level and will pre-fill target weights automatically. The first session is just calibration.
NUTRITION INFO — READ FIRST
General
These are estimates based on population-level formulas. Individual metabolism varies. Use as a starting point, adjust after 2–3 weeks based on real progress.
High Protein Diets
High protein intake (2g+/kg) is safe for healthy individuals and well-supported by research. However, if you have existing kidney disease or reduced kidney function, high protein intake can accelerate progression. Consult a doctor before starting a high-protein diet if you have any renal concerns.
Carbohydrates & Blood Sugar
High carbohydrate targets (50–60% of calories) can impact blood glucose levels. If you have type 1 or type 2 diabetes, insulin resistance, or metabolic syndrome, do not follow high-carb targets without medical supervision. Endurance plans in particular use very high carb ratios — these are intended for people with healthy insulin function.
Dietary Fats & Cardiovascular Health
Fat targets here include all dietary fat. If you have cardiovascular disease, high LDL cholesterol, or a family history of heart disease, the type of fat you eat matters significantly. Prioritise unsaturated fats (olive oil, nuts, avocado, fatty fish). Limit saturated and trans fats regardless of the total fat target shown.
Caloric Deficits
A 400 kcal/day deficit is moderate and generally safe for healthy adults. Deficits larger than 500 kcal/day over extended periods increase risk of muscle loss, hormonal disruption, and micronutrient deficiency. Do not exceed the recommended deficit without professional guidance.
This app does not provide medical or dietary advice. All recommendations are for informational purposes only. If you have any medical condition, consult a registered dietitian or physician before making significant dietary changes.
